Strain-life fatigue (ε–N)

The strain-life route uses cyclic material parameters to relate stabilized total strain amplitude to reversals to failure. OpenModel separates local elastic-to-strain conversion from the strain-life damage model.

1. Coffin–Manson–Basquin material parameters

The current model stores Young's modulus \(E\), fatigue strength coefficient \(\sigma_f'\), fatigue strength exponent \(b\), fatigue ductility coefficient \(\varepsilon_f'\) and fatigue ductility exponent \(c\). The implemented validation requires

\[E>0,\quad \sigma_f'>0,\quad \varepsilon_f'>0,\quad b<0,\quad c<0.\]

The independent variable is reversals to failure \(2N_f\), whereas Rainflow counts cycles. For a fully reversed stabilized cycle, the Coffin–Manson–Basquin (CMB) equation is

\[\varepsilon_a= \frac{\sigma_f'}{E}(2N_f)^b+ \varepsilon_f'(2N_f)^c.\]

The first term is the elastic (Basquin) contribution; the second is the plastic (Coffin–Manson) contribution. The supplied \(\varepsilon_a\) is total strain amplitude, not strain range or peak strain.

2. Morrow mean-stress model

Morrow modifies the elastic fatigue-strength term using the signed mean stress of the stabilized cycle:

\[\varepsilon_a= \frac{\sigma_f'-\sigma_m}{E}(2N_f)^b+ \varepsilon_f'(2N_f)^c.\]

In this implementation the condition \(\sigma_f'-\sigma_m>0\) is required for every cycle. Mean stress is cycle-specific, so the inverse life equation is solved separately for each cycle.

3. Smith–Watson–Topper (SWT)

The SWT parameter combines maximum tensile stress with total strain amplitude:

\[P_{\mathrm{SWT}}=\sigma_{\max}\varepsilon_a.\]

Combining it with the CMB material relation gives the life equation evaluated by the implementation:

\[P_{\mathrm{SWT}}(2N_f)= \frac{(\sigma_f')^2}{E}(2N_f)^{2b} +\sigma_f'\varepsilon_f'(2N_f)^{b+c}.\]

The input cycle must have \(\sigma_{\max}>0\). The implementation solves the right-hand side against the target \(P_{\mathrm{SWT}}\), not against strain amplitude alone.

4. Reversals, cycles and damage

After solving for reversals, the cycle life is converted to cycles:

\[N_f=\frac{2N_f}{2}.\]

For a Rainflow count weight \(n_i\), the damage increment is \(n_i/N_{f,i}\). Total strain-life Miner damage is therefore

\[D=\sum_i\frac{n_i}{N_{f,i}}.\]

As in the stress-life route, the nominal block repetition life is \(1/D\) for positive damage and \(+\infty\) for zero damage. The sum uses compensated arithmetic.

5. Numerical inversion

The CMB, Morrow and SWT equations are monotone decreasing in reversals for the required negative exponents and positive coefficients (with the Morrow coefficient condition satisfied). OpenModel brackets the solution on the caller's configured interval and performs bisection in logarithmic coordinates:

\[x=\log_{10}(2N_f),\qquad x\in[\log_{10}N_{\min}^{(r)},\log_{10}N_{\max}^{(r)}].\]

It performs 160 bisection iterations. The defaults are 1 and \(10^{16}\) reversals. If the target parameter lies outside the curve values at the interval endpoints, the cycle is rejected. There is no life extrapolation outside this interval.

6. Local cyclic stress–strain relation

\[\varepsilon_a=\frac{\sigma_a}{E} +\left(\frac{\sigma_a}{K'}\right)^{1/n'},\]

Neuber rule

For elastic FEM stress amplitude \(\sigma_{a,e}\), Neuber imposes

\[\sigma_a\varepsilon_a=\frac{\sigma_{a,e}^2}{E}.\]

Substituting Ramberg–Osgood produces a scalar nonlinear equation in \(\sigma_a\):

\[\sigma_a\left[ \frac{\sigma_a}{E}+ \left(\frac{\sigma_a}{K'}\right)^{1/n'} \right]-\frac{\sigma_{a,e}^2}{E}=0.\]

Glinka energy method

Glinka equates the local cyclic strain-energy density to the elastic energy density:

\[W(\sigma_a)=\int_0^{\sigma_a}\sigma\,d\varepsilon =\frac{\sigma_{a,e}^2}{2E}.\]

The standard strain-energy integral for the Ramberg–Osgood law is

\[W(\sigma_a)=\int_0^{\varepsilon_a}\sigma\,d\varepsilon =\frac{\sigma_a^2}{2E} +\frac{K'}{1+n'} \left(\frac{\sigma_a}{K'}\right)^{1+1/n'}.\]
Implementation note: LocalStrainCorrection evaluates the plastic term as \(\sigma_a\varepsilon_{p}/(1+n')\), i.e. the strain-energy integral \(\int_0^{\varepsilon_a}\sigma\,d\varepsilon\) above. Earlier revisions used the complementary integral \(\int_0^{\sigma_a}\varepsilon\,d\sigma\) (denominator \(1+1/n'\)), which over-predicted local strain, even above the Neuber result. For \(n'<1\) Glinka now returns a smaller local strain than Neuber.

Both the Neuber and the Glinka equations use bounded monotone bisection with finite-value checks. The search upper bound starts at the elastic amplitude and is doubled until the response brackets the target.

Tabulated versus parametric data: FatigueCurve can store a tabulated StrainLifeEN family, but StrainLifeDamageModel currently evaluates the CMB parameter set above. It does not query that tabulated curve family.

7. End-to-end FEM strain-life orchestration

StrainLifeAnalysis::run executes the stages in order:

  1. Build a stress tensor history from the same field and FEM position at every physical-time step.
  2. Project the tensor using the explicit unit direction and count scalar Rainflow cycles.
  3. Convert elastic cycle amplitudes with Neuber or Glinka.
  4. Evaluate CMB, Morrow or SWT life and accumulate Miner damage.

The orchestrator currently requires a stress history; it does not reinterpret an FEM strain field as a stress history or derive local stabilized stress/strain loops from an incremental plasticity calculation. It also requires the FEM stress unit and the two material stress units to match exactly, the strain units to match exactly, and the two material records to use exactly the same Young's modulus.

The local correction retains the supplied elastic cycle mean stress and approximates maximum stress as \(\sigma_{\max}=\sigma_m+\sigma_a\). It does not model residual-stress relaxation, ratcheting, cyclic hardening/softening, non-proportional loading or hysteresis-loop reconstruction. Material constants must be calibrated for the relevant temperature, environment, orientation and cyclic condition; temperature is metadata here, not an automatic material-property interpolation.
