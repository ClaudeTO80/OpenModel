Numerics and worked examples

How the nonlinear equations are solved, what accuracy and cost were measured, and end-to-end examples whose numbers were produced by the library and cross-checked independently. The values were obtained by running the library, with an independent re-computation in Python for examples A and B.

1. The common two-term equation

Coffin–Manson–Basquin, Morrow, SWT, Wang–Brown and the Neuber/Glinka local rules all reduce to finding \(x>0\) such that

\[ f(x)=P\,x^{p}+Q\,x^{q}=T,\qquad P,Q,T>0,\quad p,q\neq0\ \text{of equal sign}. \]
Equation\(x\)\(P\)\(p\)\(Q\)\(q\)\(T\)
CMB\(2N_f\)\(\sigma_f'/E\)\(b\)\(\varepsilon_f'\)\(c\)\(\varepsilon_a\)
Morrow\(2N_f\)\((\sigma_f'-\sigma_m)/E\)\(b\)\(\varepsilon_f'\)\(c\)\(\varepsilon_a\)
SWT\(2N_f\)\(\sigma_f'^2/E\)\(2b\)\(\sigma_f'\varepsilon_f'\)\(b+c\)\(\sigma_{\max}\varepsilon_a\)
Wang–Brown\(2N_f\)\(C_1\sigma_f'/E\)\(b\)\(C_2\varepsilon_f'\)\(c\)\(\gamma_{\max}/2+S\Delta\varepsilon_n\)
Neuber\(\sigma\)\(1/E\)2\(K'^{-1/n'}\)\(1+1/n'\)\(\sigma_e^2/E\)
Glinka\(\sigma\)\(1/(2E)\)2\(K'^{-1/n'}/(1+n')\)\(1+1/n'\)\(\sigma_e^2/(2E)\)

With \(u=\ln x\) the function \(h(u)=\ln f(e^u)-\ln T\) is a log-sum-exp of two affine functions: convex and strictly monotone. The solver (src/fatigue/TwoTermSolver.hpp, internal) works as follows:

  1. The coefficients are kept as logarithms (\(\ln P,\ln Q\)), so terms like \(K'^{-1/n'}\) with \(n'=0.05\) (an exponent of −20) cannot overflow before use.
  2. The search interval \([u_{\rm low},u_{\rm high}]\) is the logarithm of the configured reversal bounds (strain-life) or \([\ln\sigma_e+10^{-6}-60,\ \ln\sigma_e+10^{-6}]\) (local rules; the solution never exceeds the elastic amplitude).
  3. The target must be bracketed: \(h\) must change sign across the interval. Otherwise the call fails and the caller reports the cycle as outside the interval (strain-life) or falls back to bisection (local rules).
  4. Newton iterations \(u\leftarrow u-h/h'\) start from the end where \(h\ge0\). If the Newton point leaves the current bracket, or the step does not at least halve the previous step, a bisection step is taken instead (the safeguard of the classical rtsafe routine). The result therefore cannot be worse than bisection and is quadratically convergent near the root.
  5. Termination: \(|\Delta u|\le 8\varepsilon_{\rm mach}\max(1,|u|)\) or bracket narrower than the same bound; at most 200 iterations.

The earlier implementation used 160 bisection steps per cycle on a fixed interval. The new one is a drop-in replacement; the roots agree to rounding (differential tests over random parameter sets).

These are counts of function evaluations in a micro-benchmark on one machine; wall-clock gains depend on the cost of exp/log and on how much of an analysis is spent in the solver (for tensor histories read from large result files, I/O and Rainflow dominate).

Accuracy. The solver tolerance is at rounding level in \(\ln x\), i.e. a relative error in life of about \(10^{-15}\times|\ln x|\) in exact arithmetic. The practical accuracy is set by the input data, which is typically known to a few percent at best; life scatter in fatigue is a factor of 2–10. Do not read significance into digits beyond the third.

3. Worked example A — stress-life with Rainflow and Miner

Uniaxial elastic stress history at one node, in MPa, at times 0…7:

\[ \sigma(t)=0,\ 800,\ 100,\ 700,\ 0,\ 900,\ 100,\ 0. \]

S–N curve at 293.15 K (stress amplitude in MPa, life in cycles): \((300,10^6),(400,2\cdot10^5),(500,5\cdot10^4),(600,10^4)\).

The interpolated life at 450 MPa is \(\log_{10}N=4.9832\) (between the 400 and 500 MPa points). Summing gives

\[ D=1.6393\times10^{-5},\qquad L_{\rm block}=1/D\approx6.100\times10^{4}\ \text{repetitions}. \]

Note that 800→100→700→0 contains a closed inner cycle 100–700 (range 600), which is counted as a full cycle; the rest are half cycles, so the total counted weight is 3.0 (one full cycle plus four half cycles).

Goodman with \(S_u=900\) MPa on the same cycles. The first cycle becomes \(300/(1-400/900)=540\) MPa, \(N\approx2.53\times10^4\), but the 400 MPa-mean cycles of amplitude 400 become 720 MPa, which is above the highest tabulated amplitude (600 MPa). The evaluation therefore fails with CurveEvaluationFailed (status 4) rather than returning a partial sum. Extending the table is the remedy, and it must be a deliberate decision: Goodman-equivalent amplitudes above the data are extrapolations.

Below the curve. A cycle with amplitude 150 MPa is outside \([300,600]\) and also fails (AmplitudeOutOfRange inside the curve, CurveEvaluationFailed at damage level), with the diagnostic "Amplitude is outside the common interpolation range; extrapolation is disabled." For typical histories with many small cycles, add a sub-limit point to the curve; otherwise the whole position fails.

The same numbers for an S–N curve with temperature slices follow by interpolating \(\log_{10}N\) linearly in kelvin between the bracketing slices; evaluating the example curve at 350 K when the only slice is 293.15 K returns TemperatureOutOfRange (status 9).

Material: \(E=210\,000\) MPa, \(K'=1000\) MPa, \(n'=0.15\); \(\sigma_f'=1000\) MPa, \(b=-0.10\), \(\varepsilon_f'=0.20\), \(c=-0.50\). One cycle with elastic amplitude \(\sigma_e=300\) MPa and elastic mean 50 MPa, count 1:

Observations: (1) the mean stress of +50 MPa shortens the life by about 21% (CMB→Morrow) and about 53% (CMB→SWT) in this example; (2) the choice Neuber versus Glinka changes the life by 36% here, more than the choice between CMB and Morrow, and the difference grows with plasticity (factor 2.2 at \(\sigma_e=600\) MPa); (3) SWT is the most conservative of the three for this tensile mean.

5. Worked example C — Wang–Brown

Same strain-life constants, \(S=0.30\), \(\nu_e=0.3\), \(\nu_p=0.499\): \(C_1=1+0.3+0.7\cdot0.3=1.510\), \(C_2=1+0.499+0.501\cdot0.3=1.6493\). A cycle with \(\gamma_{\max}/2=0.004\) and \(\Delta\varepsilon_n=0.006\) has damage parameter \(0.004+0.3\cdot0.006=0.0058\), giving \(D=1.6462\times10^{-4}\) per cycle, i.e. a life of 6075 cycles. With \(S=0\) and no normal-strain excursion (pure shear cycle) the same shear amplitude gives \(D=7.047\times10^{-5}\) (life about 14 190 cycles): non-proportionality with this calibration shortens the life by a factor of about 2.3. These values only illustrate the equations; the constants are not those of a real material.

The relative strain between the zero state and \(\varepsilon_{xx}=0.002,\ \varepsilon_{yy}=\varepsilon_{zz}=-0.0006,\ \varepsilon_{xy}=0.001\) is 0.002403 for \(\nu=0.3\) and 0.002083 for \(\nu\to0.5\): the effective Poisson ratio rescales the measure by \((1+\nu)\) and matters at the 15% level.

6. Output formatting and reproducibility

  • CSV and JSON writers write every double with 17 significant digits (exact round trip) and always use . as the decimal point and no digit grouping, regardless of the locale imbued in the output stream or of the global C++ locale. The stream's locale, precision and format flags are restored when the writer returns.
  • CSV text fields are RFC 4180 quoted with embedded quotes doubled, so labels or diagnostics containing commas, quotes or newlines parse back unchanged.
  • Results are deterministic: no threads, no randomness, and sums use Kahan compensation. Results can still differ in the last digits between compilers or CPUs because of different exp/log/pow implementations and fused multiply-add contraction.

7. Known limitations of this documentation

  • Numbers on this page come from a build with GCC on Linux x86-64. The code was not built with MSVC for this review.
  • The methods are described to the extent the code implements them; no claim is made that they reproduce results of any commercial fatigue package, and no validation against test data of a real component was performed.
  • Typical-range remarks (e.g. \(n'\approx0.1\)–0.2, \(b\approx-0.05\) to \(-0.12\)) are general engineering experience, not values enforced or verified by the library.
